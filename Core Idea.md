# From Ash

## What are we aiming to solve?

Structureless Superflat Survival in Minecraft is an extremely hard, yet rewarding, challenge. It requires deep knowledge of game mechanics to go from a basically unplayable state to a sprawling civilization which, sadly, will forever lack some forms of technology and materials. This mod does not seek to allow players to encounter items or structures that would fundamentally break the experience, such as allowing for players to obtain Lava (which can also open possibilities such as the Nether), or any means of getting to the End dimension.

This mod is fundamentally about allowing for certain materials to exist within the context of the game. 

## What do we have to work with in Vanilla?
The world's base materials include:
- Dirt (lots of it)
- Mob Drops
  - Bones dropped from Skeletons
  - Iron ingots dropped from Zombies
  - Copper ingots dropped from Drowned
  - Redstone and Glowstone dropped from Witches
  - Wool dropped from Sheep or crafted from String dropped from Spiders

As well as an assortment of other items dropped by passive and hostile mobs alike.

### Phase 1 -- The Break

While not strictly required, a pathfinding mob farm can definitely help you survive for longer, and will also produce important resources that can be used later on. There are a handful of designs that use only dirt for the most basic necessities, or some combination of dirt and wool carpets. While building your farm, and while you use it, await for the wandering trader to appear. Wandering traders expand the possibilities of the world by allowing trades for Milk Buckets (which requires killing a WT and having decent drop luck) as well as Baked Potatoes (Which require killing burning Zombies, either with daylight or a Flame-enchanted Bow; this requires 4 Baked Potatoes for a single Emerald); a single emerald can bring in either a set of 8 logs or a pair of Moss Blocks, which will open up 3x3 crafting and allow the player for a number of paths. Upon gathering items through your method of choice, find a Wandering Trader who has the trades available to you, and grab an emerald in return. In the meantime, though, be sure to get at least 7 (but preferably 10) Iron Ingots.

### Phase 2 -- Something From Nothing

#### The 8-log Set Path

This may be a tough break, but also a way to blow the challenge wide open; the log trade is a guarantee for every WT. Starting by taking a single log and transforming it into a crafting table, probably the most important item for this challenge to become viable. A crafting table will allow you to create a bed (which will consume a second log in the form of 3 out of the 4 planks), which will help you with hostile mobs and surviving the night, and then will also allow you to create a hoe (which will take up another log). With this hoe, you will be able to farm for Wheat, which can be turned into Hay Bales, and these can also be traded with the Wandering Trader (if you are lucky to receive such a trade).
The crafting table also allows you to create a cauldron to collect rainwater, as well as buckets to collect said water. Wait for enough time to pass, and you will be able to produce a water source seemingly out of nowhere; this source can then be used in a number of ways to be duplicated, allowing for an infinite water source, as well as two more potential trades with WT: water bottles (with bottles collected from witches) and water buckets.
You might also want to create a chest to store surplus items, or valuables, but this is optional. Upon obtaining more items, make sure to grab the first available sapling - you can acquire other types later.

#### The Azalea Path

If you are lucky to receive a Moss trade from your WT, count your blessings - despite this wood type requiring bone meal to be grown, it is renewable and essentially infinite. This has no drawbacks over the other path, other than the moss trade not being a guarantee. You can do the same things described in the other path, with the added bonus of potentially being less mindful of your wood usage.


### Phase 3 -- Breathing Life Back Into The World

With an infinite water source acquired, you can look for ways to adapt your mob farm (you should have built one by this point - if not, do it now) and generate Drowned. These Drowned, when killed, have a slight chance of dropping copper; this becomes a similar challenge to acquiring iron, but this time around you only need 3 for a lightning rod. Make sure to also farm for carrots/potatoes to breed pigs; you will need A LOT of them. Place the lightning rod among the pigs, then await for a thunderstorm. Once a lightning bolt hits the rod, the pigs will be transformed into Zombie Piglins, which can be killed for their gold bars and ingots.
In the meantime, amass enough resources for buying an Oak/Dark Oak sapling with WT. When you acquire it, make sure to save the apples that will eventually drop, and make sure to keep at least two.
Combine these resources to create an Enchanted Apple, and set it aside. 

Finally, trap a pair of Zombie Villagers, and with the help of a trapped witch, hit them with a splash potion of Weakness, then cure them with the golden apples. 

### Phase 4 -- Industrialize

Given you will now have two Villagers, your priority should be to build a breeder, so that you will have a surplus of traders. With these Villagers, the final possible Vanilla blocks will become available; also, Iron Farms become viable. We will still be locked out of stone and lava, so our building options are limited by having about only half of the potential professions.

### Phase 5 -- Now what?

We would like to build with what we have. And despite now having access to a lot of items, we potentially could have more. For example - we have a type of stone in the dripstone block, which can be acquired after trading regular dripstone with a WT, growing it with water, and grabbing that and turning it into a block. We would also have access to some more blocks if we were able to process clay (either smelting clay balls into clay bricks, or full blocks for terracotta), which can be obtained by turning dirt into mud and then using a dripstone beneath it; this could also bring in a furnace, such as the one from the "Brick Furnace" mod. We could maybe turn the dirt into something else (or even the mud into some new type of "fired mud"). We could have more patterns for wood-related blocks, or maybe use campfires to get ash (and use it similarly to the AshClay mod, which combines wood ash and water to generate "Ash Clay Bricks" that can also be made into a furnace). We could use more decoration items, such as the "Macaw" mod suite which has loads of doors, windows, bridges, and many other things. How could we create a mod that doesn't break the immersion of Structureless Superflat while breathing fresh air into it? Sort of like a "Vanilla+".
